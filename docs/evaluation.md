# Evaluation

Generated by `python -m pm.evaluate`: 150 simulated machines run to failure (90 to train, 30 to calibrate,
30 held out), 24,270+ vibration windows, four failure modes. The data is simulated, so this shows the methods recover
planted wear patterns; it does not show performance on real machines.

## 1. Remaining useful life

Scored on the held-out machines, in their last 300 hours (where a wrong answer costs money).

| | Mean absolute error |
|---|---|
| Quantile gradient boosting, median (`pdm-1.0.0`) | 23.0 h |
| Baseline: average life of the fleet minus the machine's age | 180.7 h |

The 10-90% band should hold the true value 80% of the time. Straight from the models it held 75.5%; after conformal calibration on machines
the models never saw (widening by 5.0 h each side) it held 80.7%. Mean band width in the last 300 hours: 138.8 h.

## 2. Anomaly detection

Three alarms, each set to the same false-alarm rate (0.5% of healthy windows on the calibration machines) and required to fire on two windows in a row.
Warning time = hours of life left when the alarm first fires; a machine that never alarms counts as zero.

| Alarm | Median warning | Machines never alarmed | Machines alarmed while still healthy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance from the machine's own healthy baseline, five indicators together | 773 h | 0 of 30 | 0 |
| Overall vibration level (RMS) against its healthy baseline | 535 h | 0 of 30 | 0 |
| Isolation forest on the same five indicators | 167 h | 12 of 30 | 0 |

| Failure mode | Distance | RMS level | Isolation forest |
|---|---|---|---|
| outer race | 863 h | 599 h | 317 h |
| imbalance | 828 h | 706 h | 0 h |
| misalignment | 822 h | 474 h | 0 h |
| lubrication | 605 h | 395 h | 191 h |

The isolation forest was the blueprint's suggested baseline and the first thing built. It failed in an instructive way: its score saturates. A point far outside
the healthy cloud is isolated in about as few splits as a point just outside it, so a badly worn machine scored no higher than the odd healthy outlier, and
12 of 30 machines never crossed a threshold set for a 0.5% false-alarm rate. A plain distance from the healthy baseline keeps growing as wear grows.

## 3. Failure-mode classifier

On windows from the last 40% of life of held-out machines: 100.0% correct across four modes (guessing: 25%).
The four simulated modes leave cleanly different fingerprints (1x shaft speed, 2x shaft speed, the bearing defect line, high-frequency noise), which is why this is near-perfect here
and would not be on real machines, where faults overlap.

## Simulator

Windows are 4096 samples at 8192 Hz every 6 hours. Wear grows with the cube of the share of life used. Shaft-rate tones vary with a random load
factor (15%) from window to window, which is what makes a plain level alarm noisy.
